E14 Statement:

The Shell Eco-Marathon is an international contest in which teams compete with different fuel systems to see who can make the most fuel efficient vehicle. For the next generation of Swarthmore’s competition vehicle, there is an anticipated use of wrapped carbon fiber tubes to construct the chassis. To join these tubes together, a combination of 3D printed tube holders and aluminum insert backbones will be used to support loads of up to 200 lbs of tension and compression within members. The easiest way to make a vehicle more efficient is to reduce weight, so we are looking to shave off weight wherever safe and possible.

The goal of this lab is to estimate the approximate modulus of elasticity of the combined system of the wrapped carbon fiber with the aluminum running on the inside to reinforce the joint, and to compare the modulus of elasticity of the beam to the individual elements. This will help inform if more weight can be reduced for these joints, and, if so, how much.

You will be provided with 6 separate test configurations to perform a 3 point bending test in the Universal Testing Machine (UTM): 12” of unaltered wrapped carbon fiber tube, 12” of unaltered aluminum rod, 2x6” members of wrapped carbon fiber tube with full thickness aluminum inside, 2x6” members of wrapped carbon fiber tube with the .25” diameter of aluminum drilled out, 2x6” members of wrapped carbon fiber tube with the .375” diameter of aluminum drilled out, 2x6” members of wrapped carbon fiber tube with the .5” diameter of aluminum drilled out.

Displacement=PL^3/(48EI)

P=Load,L=Length of full member, E=Modulus of Elasticity, I=Moment of Inertia

I=(½)M(R1^2+R2^2)
