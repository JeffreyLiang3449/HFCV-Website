Engineering Design Project Proposal

Hydrogen Fuel-Cell Vehicle

Swarthmore College Engineering

December 2024

By Michael Moran Bojorquez ’25, Gabriel Salas Carrillo ’25

Advisor: Carr Everbach

Abstract

This project aims to continue the revitalisation efforts of the Hydrogen Fuel-Cell Vehicle through its redesign and reconstruction using the data and progress made in the past spring and summer. The ultimate goal of the project is to complete a vehicle fit for the 2025 Shell-Eco Marathon competition. This work will provide key experience in vehicle designing with an emphasis in optimization and fuel-efficiency. It will also further the work into alternative fuel sources and their effectiveness. The project will be accomplished through a mix of literature review, design planning, manufacturing, assembly, and testing. All to create the most safe, simple to drive, and fuel-efficient vehicle possible within the project’s time. This project also continues the previous goal of attracting interest and participants to the Hydrogen Fuel-Cell vehicle effort and the creation of a competitive team to annually enter the Shell-Eco Marathon.

Introduction

Climate change has progressively become a significant crisis in today's world due to a concerning increase in the amount of carbon dioxide emissions. Gasoline powered vehicles are a major contributor to the acceleration of global warming, leading to extreme weather conditions, rising sea levels, and other economical concerns. The automotive sector being a big contributor has resulted in the advent transition into renewable energy sources, one of them being hydrogen powered vehicles. This greener alternative is a promising solution and a great alternative to gasoline and diesel engines. Through the usage of hydrogen powered vehicles, it can be expected that the contribution to climate change would be lowered.

To participate in the efforts for research into using hydrogen as a fuel source, our team aims to continue the work of alumni in the development of a hydrogen fuel-cell powered vehicle with a focus on fuel efficiency and safety. In order to guide the development of the vehicle, the goal of the project is to meet the standards for participation in the Shell-Eco Marathon which is a worldwide fuel efficiency competition and which the college has participated in, in previous years. The current vehicle is outdated and guaranteed to fail the inspection section of the competition. It is also difficult to drive and control while also being quite uncomfortable for the driver. To address this, our teams’ main efforts will be concentrated on the construction of the new and lighter chassis, the integration of a hydraulic disk brake system to the front wheels, integration of a new suspension system, development of a new steering system, and the possible development of a new cowl and new protective cage as well as other miscellaneous components that might prove necessary during the development of the vehicle. While chances of participating in the upcoming 2025 Shell-Eco Marathon competition in April are slim, our team will put forth the effort to try and have the vehicle ready. Ultimately, this project will at least make progress in the development of the vehicle and give underclassmen a greater chance for success in the competition.

Technical Discussion

Swarthmore College has a long history of participating in the Shell Eco-Marathon, but the evolving competition rules have rendered the current vehicle design outdated and inadequate to compete. As of today, the vehicle requires significant modifications, including the front suspensions, rack and pinion steering system, hydraulic brakes, soundness and rigidity of the vehicle's chassis, and the redesign of the cowl, in order to meet the latest safety and performance standards. The vehicle has undergone multiple modifications, and has yet to meet the standards to qualify. Given this, the team has decided to completely redesign the vehicle in an effort to reach the competition. The design of the new vehicle will be mostly new, only taking inspiration from past designs and using some of the changes made by the previous senior group. Our new design aims to address previous shortcomings to set a stage for future development. The following sections will explore the mechanics for critical components of the HFCV and their implications for the design.

Competition

The Shell-Eco Marathon is a competition based on fuel efficiency. The main events of the competition are individual races with a time limit. After each run the fuel consumption of the vehicle is measured. As such, the competition is not a conventional race in the sense that time and speed are not of much importance. Instead, the focus lies in the efficiency of the vehicle and its mileage per gallon of hydrogen. This is why a goal of this project is to reduce the overall weight of the vehicle, as this would lead to a reduction in fuel consumption.

New Chassis

The current chassis of the vehicle is composed of aluminum beams and plates, making it quite heavy and inefficient. The new proposed chassis will be composed of mostly carbon fiber parts and aluminum joints with 3D printed collars. This decision comes from the results found by the 2024 senior team on the effectiveness and benefits of each material they tested. With wrapped carbon fiber and 3D printed collars being the most fuel efficient and resistant materials. The new chassis will follow the previous design, but as a result of the new materials used it will end up being slightly larger. This will be beneficial for the project as it is also planned to integrate a second floor to the chassis to cover the steering system and provide more comfort and safety to the driver.

Braking

The Shell-Eco Marathon regulations require that the vehicle have a hydraulic braking system for both the front and rear axles. Work done on the vehicle during the spring of 2024 by previous seniors included the integration of hydraulic brakes to the rear wheel. The previous team opted to use a hydraulic disk braking system due to their stopping power and efficient heat dissipation. As such, our team will continue using this kind of braking system due to the advantages it provides and also because of the documentation made by the previous team, which should save some of our precious time.

A hydraulic disk braking system involves the use of a master cylinder, brake calipers, hoses/lines, and disk rotors. The system works by applying force to the master cylinder which compresses the brake fluid and creates hydraulic pressure. This pressure is transported through the hoses and into the brake calipers, which contain two small pistons on each side. The pressure from the brake fluid causes the pistons to move and exert pressure on the disk rotor, which ultimately causes the wheel to stop turning.

It is important to make sure that the master cylinder and the brake calipers are compatible with each other. The amount of pressure exerted by the master cylinder is heavily dependent on its bore diameter as it is what determines the amount of brake fluid that the cylinder can displace. [1] A larger bore diameter means more fluid displaced which means more pressure acting on the pistons of the brake calipers. This can create more braking power, but it is possible that the pressure can overwhelm the calipers if they are not designed for it and cause damage and wear in the calipers and/or the disk rotor. An opposite effect could also happen. If the bore size is too small it might be unable to create enough pressure to effectively move the pistons in the calipers, and thus reduce the stopping power. Another aspect of note is that the synergy of the master cylinder and the brake calipers can impact the feel of the brake, making it feel too heavy or too light if the components are incompatible. It is for these reasons that it is often recommended to use a hydraulic disk brake system with components from the same brand, as they are designed to be compatible. This is what the previous team of seniors did with the brakes for the rear wheel of the vehicle.

The brake system implemented in the rear wheel has the master cylinder in the steering wheel and it uses a lever to activate it, while the disk rotor and brake caliper are attached to the motor integrated to the wheel. This previous work will be used to guide some of the implementation of a similar braking system with the front wheels. More specifically, the integration of the disk rotors to the wheels. However, a new master cylinder will need to be selected as the competition regulations require that the front wheel brakes be activated through a pedal.

Mechanics of a Rack and Pinion Steering System

As stated in the competition rules, under Article 32 Steering, it is essential for the vehicle to have a modern steering mechanism that must allow for both hands to be used when driving. [2] This is done to increase the overall safety of vehicles and to provide a modern update. Through the development of a modern steering system it ensures proofness for competition standards and future development. The mechanical principle of a rack and pinion steering system converts a steering wheel's rotational motion into linear motion, that is needed to turn a vehicle’s wheels. [3] The pinion, which consists of circular gears that mesh with a flat toothed component known as the rack, is what allows for the circular motion to be converted into linear motion. When the steering wheel is rotated the pinion rotates, moving the rack latterly either to the left or right. The lateral movement is then transferred through tie rods to the steering wheel, causing the wheels to pivot accordingly in the direction intended. [3] Power assisted systems, such as a hydraulic power steering (HPS) reduces the drive’s required work by providing additional force to the rack's internal motion. This along with aspects such as the steering ratio and Ackermann geometry ensures optimal conditions for operation. This is pivotal according to the competition’s rules as it provides additional safety factors derived from responsive handling via smooth steering control. Together, the components combined provide a refined system that is safe and reliable.

Requirements and Constraints

The requirements and constraints for the Hydrogen Fuel-Cell Vehicle are defined not only by the physical constraints involved in building a vehicle, but also by the safety and regulations outlined by the Shell-Eco Marathon rules. Below is a list of the major requirements and constraints.

  • Timeline: This is a semester long project, as such, we only have approximately three and a half months to complete the design and construction of the vehicle. The project completion deadline is by the end of April 24, giving enough time to prepare a presentation and a report of the results.

  • Vehicle Dimensions Constraint: The vehicle must be within the specified dimensions for the Shell-Eco Marathon

  • Vehicle Mechanics:

    • Power - The vehicle is equipped with a 2019 model of Horizon’s H-1000 Fuel Cell stack, due to budget and design constraints this fuel cell will not be altered throughout the project. This serves as a limiting factor on the speed and power produced by the vehicle.

    • Suspencion - The Shell-Eco Marathon regulations require the integration of suspensions for each wheel of the vehicle. The suspensions were already ordered during previous year’s and summers project, so no other suspension will be used for this project.

    • Steering - The steering system has been designed, however, it has not been integrated. As such, this will be a constraint due to the need of designing the other components around the already existing steering. However, modifications for the steering system are not out of the question.

    • Hydraulic Braking - Two out of the three vehicle wheels still need the integration of a hydraulic braking system.

  • Weight Constraint: an objective of the project is to lower the weight of the vehicle by a minimum of ~50 lbs. The competition has weight restrictions that must be met.

  • Budget Constraint: As a team of two, our budget is a combined total of 800 dollars.

  • Vehicle Chassis constraint: Because of the limited time, we would be unable to develop a whole new chassis from scratch. Thus, the same chassis design will be kept. Only updating it with new materials determined by the previous senior team findings.

  • Safety Constraints: Vehicle should be able to break within a determined range and speed. Cowl and roll cage should be rigidly attached to the vehicle and not deform under pressure. Front of the vehicle must be able to crumple to a certain extent to reduce forces on the driver. A fire shield must be implemented between the driver and power cell. Vehicle must also be equipped with a fire extinguisher. Finally, the driver must be able to swiftly and safely exit the vehicle.

Plan Evaluation of Prototypes:

The goal is for the vehicle to be able to complete a lap around the outside of Singer hall. To determine if this was accomplished, multiple test runs will be conducted noting whether the vehicle completed the run or if it failed and at what point failed. The vehicle should have at least a 90% success rate. During these test runs, the vehicle will be carefully observed so that the bottom does not touch the floor anytime throughout the run. This will be the test for whether the suspension was successful or not. The steering of the vehicle will also be tested. Using the halls of Singer it will be noted whether the vehicle is able to complete a turn, its speed, and its turning ratio. This goal will be considered a success if the vehicle is able to turn within the 10m radius outlined in the competition regulations below. Finally, the vehicle should be comfortable to drive. This is tougher to quantify, but the goal is for the vehicle to be more comfortable than last year's version of it. To determine if this goal is accomplished we will ask the test drivers from last year to once again drive the vehicle and provide feedback on what they think improved.
